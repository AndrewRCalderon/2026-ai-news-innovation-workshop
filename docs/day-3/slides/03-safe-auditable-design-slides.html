Safe & Auditable Design

"Responsible AI" sounds like a value statement. It's actually a specific, checkable set of habits.

11:30 AM·45 MIN·Lecture
01

What "Auditable" Actually Means

The right first question: which actions would we need to explain later?

A minimum viable audit trail, four fields per run: what went in, what came out, what the system did with it, whether it was allowed. A spreadsheet row, not an engineering system.

02

When It Goes Wrong

NYC's MyCity chatbot
Gave false legal guidance. Kept giving the same wrong answers for over a year after being exposed, no monitoring loop caught it.
Air Canada's chatbot
Gave a wrong discount policy. Airline argued the bot was "a separate legal entity." A tribunal rejected that and made them pay.

There's no such thing as "the AI said it, not us."

03

What Real Newsrooms Require

90% of 52 studied newsroom policies required disclosure. Only 8% specified how it would be enforced.

  • WBEZ: approval per instance, quarterly Oversight Committee.
  • LAist: "a human has reviewed it and that person is accountable."
  • CBC: one test question, would the audience be misled without disclosure?
04

A Working Checklist

Simplified from NIST's AI Risk Management Framework.

  • What is this for, and where shouldn't it be used?
  • What happens if it's wrong, not just if it's right?
  • Do you have a kill switch?
  • Can a user flag a wrong answer, and does anyone look?

🎯 Apply This

Description: Answer the four questions for your own project. Any "no" is worth fixing before Final Coding Time.
