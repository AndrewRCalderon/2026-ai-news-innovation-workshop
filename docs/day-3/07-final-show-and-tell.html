01

Format

Same format as Day 2's show and tell, one more round. A few minutes each, everyone presents, everyone gives feedback.

  • Show what you built, live if you can.
  • Say what changed since Day 2's show and tell, and why.
  • Name one thing you'd build next if the workshop kept going.
02

What to Actually Say

Three days of context means the room already knows your problem statement. Skip re-explaining it. Spend your time on what's actually new: the decisions you made about the experience, and what you did (or didn't have time to do) about making it responsible.

Worth mentioning

  • A design decision from today that changed how it works
  • A place you added a human checkpoint on purpose
  • Something that broke and what you learned from it

Skip

  • Re-explaining the original problem statement
  • A feature-by-feature tour
  • Apologizing for what's unfinished, just show it
03

Closing

That's the workshop. Whatever you built this week is a starting point, not a finished product, the same way your problem statement on Day 1 was a starting point. What you actually leave with is the practice: prompting, planning, writing a spec, thinking about who's using what you build and what happens when it's wrong. That part travels with you past this week.

Keep building. The best way to get better at this is the same way you got better this week: build something, show it to people, listen to what they say, build again.
