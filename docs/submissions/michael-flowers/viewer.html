Flagged — loaded language

In the op-ed, Bessent writes this himself. A superlative asserting unprecedented scale, with no comparison offered against any previous sanctions programme. It is his claim.

Attributed — not counted

In the news report, the same words sit inside quotation marks, credited to Bessent by name. The paper is reporting the claim, not making it.

Identical words, opposite verdict — because the relationship between writer and language changed. Without this rule the news report scores 0.46 instead of 0.23, and the tool ends up penalising a newspaper for quoting a source accurately.

What this doesn't prove

  • The control is news analysis, not a wire report. Analysis carries more interpretation than a bare account of what happened, so this is a conservative comparison — a straight AP or Reuters story would likely score lower still, widening the gap rather than narrowing it.
  • One pair is not validation. This shows the detector can separate opinion from reporting on these two articles. It does not establish that it does so reliably across topics, outlets, or languages.
  • The op-ed is close to a best case. A serving cabinet secretary arguing for his own policy is unusually rich material. A subtler column would be a harder test, and is the obvious next one.
