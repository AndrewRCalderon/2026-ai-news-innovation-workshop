AI Decision Frameworks

Real, published guides from newsrooms, tech companies, and design practices on when and how to actually use AI — not workshop opinion, but people who've written this down publicly. Started while sourcing Day 1's "Use Cases" checklist; added to whenever another good one turns up.

SourceTypeWhat it offers
Google — Rules of Machine Learning Tech company Google's internal ML engineering playbook, publicly published. Rule #1: don't be afraid to launch a product without machine learning; reach for it only once a simpler approach has proven insufficient.
Google — Introduction to ML Problem Framing Tech company Criteria for judging whether the data behind an ML/AI system is good enough to trust: reliable, correct, representative, and from a source you understand.
Associated Press — Generative AI Standards Newsroom AP's newsroom policy: treat all generative AI output as unvetted source material — a journalist must apply normal sourcing standards before using any of it.
Trusting News — AI Trust Kit Newsroom / research Audience research (not just theory) finding that AI use, even disclosed carefully, measurably lowers reader trust — the credibility stakes of an AI mistake are real, not hypothetical.
IDEO — AI Ethics Cards Design practice A discussion-prompt deck built for design teams evaluating AI use, built around the line: "just because AI can do something doesn't mean that it should."
Wilson & Daugherty (Accenture) — "Collaborative Intelligence," Harvard Business Review Tech company Widely-cited framework on human-AI collaboration: the biggest gains come from AI extending human judgment and creativity, not substituting for them. Note: full text is paywalled past the abstract.
