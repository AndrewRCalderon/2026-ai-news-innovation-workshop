Prompting

"Prompt engineering" used to mean tricks. Increasingly, it just means being clear about what you want.

01

How This Has Changed

What counted as good practice right after ChatGPT launched looks pretty different from what Anthropic recommends now.

  1. 2022

    A craft already forming

    Learn Prompting launched in October 2022, before ChatGPT existed.

  2. Dec 2022

    The role-play era

    "Awesome ChatGPT Prompts" popularized assigning Claude a persona: "act as a linux terminal," "act as an English translator."

  3. 2023

    A six-figure job title

    "Prompt engineer" postings reportedly reached $335,000.

  4. 2024

    Claude starts writing prompts for you

    Anthropic shipped a Prompt Generator, then a Prompt Improver.

  5. 2025

    Less scaffolding, more curation

    Anthropic: many earlier tricks matter less now.

02

What Works Now

The best prompt isn't the longest or the most cleverly structured. It's the one that gets you what you want with the least unnecessary scaffolding.

Say what you want, give context Claude can't already see, and skip elaborate persona framing unless it's doing something for the task. A prompt that reads like it's trying to trick the model is a sign it's out of date, not thorough.

03

Using Claude to Refine Your Prompt

If a prompt isn't landing, asking Claude to improve it is a legitimate move, not a shortcut around doing the work.

Generate
Describe what you're trying to do, Claude drafts a first version.
Improve
Hand Claude a prompt you already wrote and ask it to strengthen it.
