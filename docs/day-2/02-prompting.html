01

How This Has Changed

Three years is a long time in this field. What counted as good practice right after ChatGPT launched looks pretty different from what Anthropic recommends now.

  1. 2022

    A craft already forming

    Learn Prompting, one of the first dedicated prompting guides, launched in October 2022, before ChatGPT existed.*

  2. Dec 2022

    The role-play era

    A week after ChatGPT's launch, "Awesome ChatGPT Prompts" popularized a style built on assigning Claude a persona: "I want you to act as a linux terminal," "I want you to act as an English translator." * This kind of framing, plus heavy structural scaffolding, was the dominant advice for years.

  3. 2023

    A six-figure job title

    By spring 2023, "prompt engineer" was a real, well-paid job, with postings reportedly as high as $335,000.* Even then, not everyone was convinced it would last as a distinct specialty.

  4. 2024

    Claude starts writing prompts for you

    Anthropic shipped a Prompt Generator, describing what you want and letting Claude draft the prompt,* followed months later by a Prompt Improver that strengthens a prompt you already wrote.*

  5. 2025

    Less scaffolding, more curation

    By late 2025, Anthropic's own guidance was explicit: many earlier tricks matter less now.*

02

What Works Now

The best prompt isn't the longest or the most cleverly structured. It's the one that gets you what you actually want with the least unnecessary scaffolding.*

Concretely: say what you want, give the context Claude can't already see (a file, a constraint, an example of what "done" looks like), and skip elaborate persona framing unless it's actually doing something for the task. If a prompt from 2023 reads like it's trying to trick the model into behaving, that's a sign it's out of date, not that it's thorough.

03

Using Claude to Refine Your Prompt

If a prompt isn't landing, asking Claude to improve it is a legitimate move, not a shortcut around doing the work. Anthropic builds this in two ways:

Generate
Describe what you're trying to do in plain language, and Claude drafts a first version of the prompt for you.
Improve
Hand Claude a prompt you already wrote and ask it to strengthen it, especially useful for a prompt that's underperforming or was written for a different model.
04

Rewrite One of Yours

🎯 Exercise: Rewrite a Day 1 Prompt

Time: ~15 minutes.

Description: Find a prompt from yesterday that didn't get you what you wanted. Rewrite it yourself first. Then, separately, ask Claude to improve your rewrite and compare the two.

Deliverable: Two versions of the same prompt, and a sentence on what actually changed between them.
