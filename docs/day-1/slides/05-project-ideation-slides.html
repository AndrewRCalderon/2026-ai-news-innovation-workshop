01

The Design Process Is Circular

The mistake people make: thinking there's a straight line from "idea" to "finished project." Reality is messy and circular.

  1. 1

    Identify a problem

    Or think you do.

  2. 2

    Ask questions

    Understand it better.

  3. 3

    Form a hypothesis

    About how to solve it.

  4. 4

    Test / prototype

    Try something concrete.

  5. 5

    Learn something

    Whatever the result tells you.

  6. 6

    Reframe the problem

    Revise your understanding.

  7. 7

    Repeat

    The whole cycle, again.

02

Finding a Problem Worth Solving

Start here

  • What frustrates you about how journalism works (or could work)?
  • What task do you do repeatedly that feels inefficient?
  • Where do you see people struggling?

Go deeper

  • Who experiences this problem?
  • Why isn't the current solution good enough?

Get specific

  • Can you describe it in one sentence?
  • Is this a real problem, or are you assuming?
03

Form a Hypothesis

A hypothesis is: "If I do X, then Y will happen."

  • "If I use AI to analyze court documents, then reporters identify patterns 10x faster."
  • "If I use AI to draft routine earnings reports, then reporters get more time for investigation."
  • "If I use AI to tag reader comments by topic, then we respond faster and improve engagement."

It has to be testable — can you try it, measure the outcome, and learn something either way?

04

Articulate Your Goal

The problem
Clear, one sentence.
The solution
What you'll actually build.
Success metric
How you'll know it worked.
"I want to solve [PROBLEM] by building [SOLUTION] so that [OUTCOME]. I'll know it works when [METRIC]."
05

Reality Check

  • Is this actually an AI problem?
  • Do you have access to the data AI would need?
  • Have you talked to people who experience this problem?
  • Is this solvable in 3 days?
  • Why hasn't someone else already built this?
06

Case Studies

Earnings reports
40% of reporter time on templated reports → an AI draft tool → 5 minutes instead of 30.
Coverage audit
Suspected uneven coverage, no data → mapped every story by neighborhood → found real gaps.
Fact-checking assistant
Verifying claims mid-interview breaks the conversation → real-time assistant → harder than expected, still in progress.

08

Key Takeaways

  • Your problem statement will evolve — that's good.
  • Talk to people. Don't solve problems for people you haven't talked to.
  • Think small.
  • AI is not always the answer.
  • Your hypothesis is a guess — it's okay to be wrong.
