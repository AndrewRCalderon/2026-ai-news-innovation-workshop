01

What Does AI Actually Do?

It's easy to get lost in abstractions. These are real, deployed use cases — not theoretical ones. Understanding what AI can actually do, and what it can't, is the difference between smart experimentation and wasting time.

02

Use Cases in Journalism

Data journalism
NYT's Cheatsheet rated 10,000 tax-registry names for investigative interest, and processed 500 hours of leaked audio in days. Catch: still verify yourself.
Routine news
The AP went from 300 to 3,000–4,000 earnings reports a quarter, freeing ~20% of reporters' time. Catch: only works for templated content.
Coverage auditing
THE CITY mapped four years of stories by neighborhood to check their own coverage gaps. Catch: verify AI's read of location and context.
Headline ideation
Ask for ten angles, pick the strongest. Catch: AI headlines skew clichéd.
Accessibility
"Explain this so a high schooler would understand." Catch: guarantees clarity, not accuracy.
03

Use Cases Outside Journalism

Customer service
Handles routine questions so people focus on real complaints. Catch: needs human oversight.
Content moderation
Flags spam for human review at scale. Catch: reflects training-data bias.
Search & recommendation
Understands intent, not just keywords. Catch: can create filter bubbles.
Video & audio
Transcribes and captions automatically. Catch: accuracy drops with noise and jargon.
Sentiment analysis
Flags where anger concentrates across comments. Catch: misreads sarcasm.
04

Not a Good Use Case (Yet)

  • Investigative reporting — can't call a source or earn a whistleblower's trust.
  • Original breaking news — synthesizes existing information; it's always behind.
  • Opinion requiring deep expertise — no credibility or unique perspective.
  • Stories requiring human experience — that reporting is human work.
  • Anything needing real-time accuracy — training data has a cutoff.
05

The Pattern

Works When

  • Repetitive and structured.
  • High-quality input data.
  • Speed matters more than perfection.
  • Augments human work.
  • You can verify the output.
  • Mistakes are tolerable.

Doesn't Work When

  • Needs original reporting.
  • Accuracy is non-negotiable.
  • Needs to replace human judgment.
  • Context and nuance are everything.
  • Readers need to trust a human call.
  • Needs real-world experience.
06

The Practical Question

  • Research & preparation: yes.
  • Analyzing datasets: yes.
  • Drafting routine content: yes, if templated.
  • Brainstorming: yes.
  • Reporting & sourcing: no.
  • Final accuracy checks: no.
  • Adding credibility: no.
Knowing which problems are AI problems and which aren't — that's the skill.
