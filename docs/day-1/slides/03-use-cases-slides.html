Use Cases

Not everything is a good AI problem. Knowing which is the real skill.

10:00 AM·30 MIN·Lecture
01

What Does AI Actually Do?

It's easy to get lost in abstractions. These are real, deployed use cases, not theoretical ones. Understanding what AI can actually do, and what it can't, is the difference between smart experimentation and wasting time.

02

Use Cases in Journalism

Data journalism
NYT's Cheatsheet rated 10,000 tax-registry names for investigative interest, and processed 500 hours of leaked audio into a story before Election Day. Catch: still verify yourself.*
Routine news
The AP went from 300 to 3,000–4,000 earnings reports a quarter, freeing ~20% of reporters' time. Catch: only works for templated content.*
Coverage auditing
THE CITY mapped four years of stories by neighborhood to check their own coverage gaps. Catch: verify AI's read of location and context.*
Headline ideation
Ask for ten angles, pick the strongest — Hearst's regional dailies built a Slack bot doing this at scale, with human review required. Catch: AI headlines skew clichéd.*
Accessibility
"Explain this so a high schooler would understand." A two-person team at Zamaneh Media used this to cut translation time from days to under an hour. Catch: guarantees clarity, not accuracy.*
02

Use Cases in Journalism (cont.)

Local government monitoring
iTromsø, a 20-person Norwegian newsroom, scans 12,000+ municipal documents a month, cutting daily research from hours to minutes. Catch: still needs editorial judgment.*
Automated claim-spotting
Chequeado's bot scans dozens of Argentine outlets and speeches to flag checkable claims — 1 in 5 weekly fact-checks now start there. Catch: still needs human verification.*
Automated local content
Swedish publisher MittMedia generates thousands of local stories a year from structured data — 61,800 in one year. Catch: only works with clean, structured data.*
Records & FOIA analysis
DocumentCloud's free AI add-ons let any newsroom summarize and search large public-records dumps, no coding required. Catch: only as good as the documents fed in.*
03

Use Cases Outside Journalism

Customer service
Handles routine questions so people focus on real complaints. Catch: needs human oversight.*
Content moderation
Flags spam for human review at scale. Catch: reflects training-data bias.*
Search & recommendation
Understands intent, not just keywords. Catch: can create filter bubbles.*
Video & audio
Transcribes and captions automatically. Catch: accuracy drops with noise and jargon.*
03

Use Cases Outside Journalism (cont.)

Sentiment analysis
Flags where anger concentrates across comments. Catch: misreads sarcasm.*
Scientific research
DeepMind's AlphaFold predicts a protein's 3D structure from its sequence, solving a 50-year "grand challenge." Catch: predictions still need lab verification.*
Legal
Lawyers submitted a ChatGPT-drafted brief citing fabricated court cases in 2023 — a federal judge sanctioned them. Catch: AI generates confident, false citations.*
Healthcare
IDx-DR became the first FDA-authorized autonomous AI diagnostic tool, screening for diabetic eye disease without a specialist. Catch: works only within a narrow, validated task.*
03

Use Cases Outside Journalism (cont.)

Software & coding
A study found about 40% of GitHub Copilot's suggested code in security-relevant scenarios had exploitable vulnerabilities. Catch: AI reproduces flaws in its training data.*
Accessibility (outside journalism)
Be My Eyes' "Be My AI," powered by GPT-4 vision, describes photographed scenes for blind and low-vision users. Catch: documented hallucinations.*
Logistics
UPS's ORION system optimizes delivery routes in real time, saving an estimated 100 million miles of driving a year. Catch: still can't match a driver's local knowledge.*
04

Not a Good Use Case (Yet)

  • Investigative reporting — can't call a source or earn a whistleblower's trust.
  • Original breaking news — synthesizes existing information; it's always behind.
  • Opinion requiring deep expertise — no credibility or unique perspective.
  • Stories requiring human experience — that reporting is human work.
  • Anything needing real-time accuracy — training data has a cutoff.
05

The Pattern

Works When

  • Repetitive and structured.
  • High-quality input data.
  • Speed matters more than perfection.
  • Augments human work.
  • You can verify the output.
  • Mistakes are tolerable.

Doesn't Work When

  • Needs original reporting.
  • Accuracy is non-negotiable.
  • Needs to replace human judgment.
  • Context and nuance are everything.
  • Readers need to trust a human call.
  • Needs real-world experience.
06

The Practical Question

Before reaching for AI, ask yourself:

  • Is the task repetitive and structured, or does it need original judgment?*
  • Is my input data trustworthy and high-quality?*
  • Can I actually verify the output myself before it goes out?*
  • Would a mistake here be tolerable, or costly to my credibility?*
  • Am I augmenting my own work, or replacing judgment I should exercise myself?*
  • Does this depend on real-time information, or human trust I can't substitute?
