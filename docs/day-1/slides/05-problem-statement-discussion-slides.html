01

The Design Process Is Circular

The mistake people make: thinking there's a straight line from "idea" to "finished project." Reality is messy and circular.* This morning, you and the group worked through the first two steps below. Now it's your turn to work through the rest.

  1. 1

    Identify a problem

    Or think you do.

  2. 2

    Ask questions

    Understand it better.

  3. 3

    Form a hypothesis

    About how to solve it.

  4. 4

    Test / prototype

    Try something concrete.

  5. 5

    Learn something

    Whatever the result tells you.

  6. 6

    Reframe the problem

    Revise your understanding.

  7. 7

    Repeat

    The whole cycle, again.

02

Finding a Problem Worth Solving

A series of questions meant to help you think.

Start here

  • What frustrates you about how you access, share, or create information?
  • What task do you do repeatedly that feels inefficient?
  • What question do you want to answer faster?
  • Where do you see people struggling?

Go deeper

  • Who experiences this problem?
  • How do they currently solve it?
  • Why isn't the current solution good enough?
  • If this problem disappeared, what would change?

Think about scale

  • How many people face this, and how often?
  • What's the cost of not solving it?
03

Get Curious with Hypothesis Testing

These are ways into a problem space, not something to nail on the first pass. We're not expecting specificity yet — we're working toward it. Forming a hypothesis, next, is one way to start closing that gap.

  • Can you describe it in one sentence?
  • Is this a real problem, or are you assuming?
04

Form a Hypothesis

A hypothesis is: "If I do X, then Y will happen."

  • "If I use AI to analyze court documents, then reporters identify patterns 10x faster."
  • "If I use AI to draft routine earnings reports, then reporters get more time for investigation."
  • "If I use AI to tag reader comments by topic, then we respond faster and improve engagement."

It has to be testable — can you try it, measure the outcome, and learn something either way?

05

Articulate Your Goal

The problem
Clear, one sentence.
The solution
What you'll actually build.
Success metric
How you'll know it worked.
"I want to solve [PROBLEM] by building [SOLUTION] so that [OUTCOME]. I'll know it works when [METRIC]."
