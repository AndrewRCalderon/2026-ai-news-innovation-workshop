01

Why Follow People

AI moves fast — new tools launch, best practices shift, and what worked last month might be outdated next month. The best way to stay informed isn't reading news articles about AI, it's following people actively working on AI, journalism, and the intersection of the two.

  • They document what they're building and why.
  • They ask hard questions about AI's impact.
  • They share concrete examples from their own reporting.
  • They're thinking ahead about what's coming next.
02

Newsroom Leaders

Zach Seward
Editorial director of AI Initiatives, The New York Times. Leads the team behind Cheatsheet; shares technical detail and editorial philosophy alike.
Rubina Madan Fillion
Associate editorial director of AI Initiatives, NYT. Focuses on the editorial side of AI deployment, not just the tech.
Marcus Whiten
Head of newsroom AI initiatives, The Washington Post. Leading one of the largest newsrooms' approach to AI integration.
Nikita Roy
ICFJ Knight Fellow, host of the Newsroom Robots podcast. Curates the most important case studies and interviews practitioners directly.
Matt Penneyacker
Program officer, Google News Initiative. Tracks what's working at scale across many newsrooms.
03

Technologists & Researchers

Jeremy Sleight
Hugging Face. Advocates for open-source AI and ethical deployment — democratizing rather than concentrating access.
Timnit Gebru
Founder, DAIR (Distributed AI Research Institute). Studies AI bias and labor impact; asks hard questions about whose interests AI actually serves.
Stuart Russell
AI safety researcher, UC Berkeley. Works on alignment — making sure AI systems do what we actually want, long-term.
04

Journalists & Practitioners

Kashmir Hill
The New York Times. Reports on the real-world impacts of AI and surveillance systems — what investigative journalism about AI looks like.
Clive Thompson
Wired, Substack. Explains tech and AI in accessible ways — a model for writing about AI for non-experts.
Charlie Warzel
The New York Times (opinion). Connects AI to the larger cultural and political questions around it.
05

Publications & Newsletters

Nieman Lab
niemanlab.org — case studies and interviews with newsroom leaders on the future of journalism. Free.
Columbia Journalism Review
cjr.org — long-form reporting on how newsrooms are using, and struggling with, AI.
The Whiteboard (Axios)
axios.com/newsletters — curated media, tech, and journalism trends.
Poynter Institute
poynter.org — practical guides on using AI in newsrooms, plus ethics guidance.
AI Index Report
aiindex.stanford.edu — annual, not journalism-specific, but the big-picture view of where AI capability is headed.
Reuters Institute
reutersinstitute.ox.ac.uk — global perspective on journalism trends, including AI adoption.
06

Communities & Events

Hashtags
#NewsroomAI, #JournalismAI (the LSE-based JournalismAI project), #AIEthics, #DataJournalism.
Conferences
JournalismAI Festival (annual, London) — direct access to practitioners. Online News Association (ONA), typically fall. Poynter Institute workshops, in-person in Tampa or online.
Open-source
Newsrooms increasingly open-source their AI tools on GitHub — search "journalism" + "AI." Hugging Face hosts thousands of free models useful for text analysis.
07

How to Use This List

Don't try to follow everyone at once. Pick 2–3 people and 1–2 newsletters to start; you'll discover other voices as you go.

What newsrooms do
Nikita Roy, Zach Seward, Nieman Lab.
Think critically
Timnit Gebru, Kashmir Hill, Charlie Warzel.
Hands-on
Poynter resources, GitHub newsroom projects, JournalismAI case studies.
Stay current
Reuters Institute reports, #NewsroomAI, Nieman Lab daily.
