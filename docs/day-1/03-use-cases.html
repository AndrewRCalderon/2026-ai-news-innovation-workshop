01

What Does AI Actually Do?

It's easy to get lost in abstractions. These are real, deployed use cases — not theoretical ones. Understanding what AI can actually do, and what it can't, is the difference between smart experimentation and wasting time.

02

Use Cases in Journalism

Data journalism
The NYT's internal tool, Cheatsheet, analyzed 10,000 names from a Puerto Rico tax-incentive registry and rated each for investigative interest — a reporter's "I can't Google 10,000 people, but a machine can." It later processed 500 hours of leaked Zoom audio (~5M words) in days instead of months. Catch: you still verify everything yourself.
Routine news
The AP went from covering 300 earnings reports a quarter to 3,000–4,000, freeing ~20% of business reporters' time for deeper analysis. Catch: only works for highly structured, templated content.
Coverage auditing
THE CITY asked ChatGPT to map four years of stories by NYC neighborhood — confirming they covered underserved areas more than they realized, while surfacing real gaps. Catch: AI misreads location and context; verify.
Headline ideation
Feed a draft or topic to Claude, ask for ten angles, pick the strongest. Catch: AI headlines skew clichéd — it's a brainstorming partner, not a final product.
Accessibility
"Explain this so a high schooler would understand," then iterate. Catch: guarantees clarity, not accuracy — you still need subject-matter expertise to fact-check.
03

Use Cases Outside Journalism

Not journalism-specific, but they apply directly:

Customer service
A chatbot handles "why was this retracted?" and "how do I subscribe?" so people can focus on real complaints. Catch: confidently wrong answers need human oversight.
Content moderation
Flags spam and off-topic comments for human review at scale. Catch: reflects training-data bias; can unfairly suppress voices without a human check.
Search & recommendation
Understands intent, not just keywords; suggests relevant follow-up reading. Catch: can create filter bubbles if not designed carefully.
Video & audio
Transcribes interviews, extracts quotes, generates captions automatically. Catch: accuracy drops with accents, noise, and jargon — proofread transcripts.
Sentiment analysis
Flags where anger or concern concentrates across thousands of comments. Catch: misreads sarcasm and irony — "great job on that terrible decision" can read as positive.
04

Not a Good Use Case (Yet)

  • Investigative reporting — AI can't call a source, dig through an archive, earn a whistleblower's trust, or read between the lines of what someone isn't saying.
  • Original breaking news — it synthesizes existing information; by the time it "knows" a story, the news is already public.
  • Opinion requiring deep expertise — AI can draft structure, but not credibility or unique perspective.
  • Stories requiring human experience — homelessness, racial justice: the reporting itself is human work.
  • Anything needing real-time accuracy — training data has a cutoff; for breaking facts, you need live sources.
05

The Pattern

Where AI actually works, and where it doesn't:

Works When

  • The task is repetitive and structured.
  • Input data is high-quality.
  • Speed matters more than perfection.
  • It augments human work, not replaces it.
  • You can verify the output yourself.
  • Mistakes are tolerable.

Doesn't Work When

  • The task needs original reporting or investigation.
  • Accuracy is non-negotiable.
  • It needs to replace human judgment.
  • Context and nuance are everything.
  • Readers need to trust a human made the call.
  • It requires real-world experience you haven't had.
06

The Practical Question

Not abstract — it's: what parts of my work can AI actually help with?

  • Research & preparation: yes, tons of opportunity.
  • Analyzing datasets: yes, AI finds patterns at scale.
  • Drafting routine content: yes, if it's templated enough.
  • Brainstorming angles and headlines: yes.
  • Reporting & sourcing: no — that's still on you.
  • Final accuracy checks: no — you can't rely on AI as a fact-checker.
  • Adding credibility: no — that comes from your reporting, not AI.
Knowing which problems are AI problems and which aren't — that's how you use this tool without letting it do the work that needs human judgment.
