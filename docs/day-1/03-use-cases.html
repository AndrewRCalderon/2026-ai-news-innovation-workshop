01

What Does AI Actually Do?

It's easy to get lost in abstractions. These are real, deployed use cases, not theoretical ones. Understanding what AI can actually do, and what it can't, is the difference between smart experimentation and wasting time.

02

Use Cases in Journalism

Data journalism
The NYT's internal tool, Cheatsheet, analyzed 10,000 names from a Puerto Rico tax-incentive registry and rated each for investigative interest — a reporter's "I can't Google 10,000 people, but a machine can." It later processed 500 hours of leaked Zoom audio (~5M words), turning an unmanageable dataset into a story before Election Day. Catch: you still verify everything yourself.*
Routine news
The AP went from covering 300 earnings reports a quarter to 3,000–4,000, freeing ~20% of business reporters' time for deeper analysis. Catch: only works for highly structured, templated content.*
Coverage auditing
THE CITY asked ChatGPT to map four years of stories by NYC neighborhood — confirming they covered underserved areas more than they realized, while surfacing real gaps. Catch: AI misreads location and context; verify.*
Headline ideation
Feed a draft or topic to Claude, ask for ten angles, pick the strongest. Hearst's regional dailies built a Slack bot doing exactly this at scale, with human review required before anything ships. Catch: AI headlines skew clichéd — it's a brainstorming partner, not a final product.*
Accessibility
"Explain this so a high schooler would understand," then iterate. A two-person team at Persian-language newsroom Zamaneh Media used this approach to cut translation time from days to under an hour. Catch: guarantees clarity, not accuracy — you still need subject-matter expertise to fact-check.*
Local government monitoring
iTromsø, a 20-person Norwegian newsroom, built an AI tool that scans 12,000+ municipal documents a month and flags newsworthy ones — cutting daily document research from hours to about 10 minutes, now used across 35 papers. Catch: still needs a reporter's judgment on what's actually a story.*
Automated claim-spotting
Argentine fact-checker Chequeado's bot scans dozens of outlets and speeches in real time to flag checkable claims — about 1 in 5 of its weekly fact-checks now start there. Catch: it flags candidates; a human still has to verify each one.*
Automated local content
Swedish publisher MittMedia uses automation to turn structured data into thousands of local real-estate and sports stories a year that reporters couldn't otherwise cover — 61,800 articles in one year at one network. Catch: only works where the underlying data is clean and structured.*
Records & FOIA analysis
DocumentCloud's free AI add-ons let any newsroom — no coding required — summarize and search large public-records dumps. Catch: built for small newsrooms without engineering staff, so it's only as good as the documents you feed it.*
03

Use Cases Outside Journalism

Not journalism-specific, but they apply directly:

Customer service
A chatbot handles "why was this retracted?" and "how do I subscribe?" so people can focus on real complaints. Catch: confidently wrong answers need human oversight.*
Content moderation
Flags spam and off-topic comments for human review at scale. Catch: reflects training-data bias; can unfairly suppress voices without a human check.*
Search & recommendation
Understands intent, not just keywords; suggests relevant follow-up reading. Catch: can create filter bubbles if not designed carefully.*
Video & audio
Transcribes interviews, extracts quotes, generates captions automatically. Catch: accuracy drops with accents, noise, and jargon — proofread transcripts.*
Sentiment analysis
Flags where anger or concern concentrates across thousands of comments. Catch: misreads sarcasm and irony — "great job on that terrible decision" can read as positive.*
Scientific research
Google DeepMind's AlphaFold predicts a protein's 3D structure from its sequence, solving a 50-year "grand challenge" in biology. Catch: predictions are computational hypotheses that still need lab verification.*
Legal
In 2023, lawyers submitted a ChatGPT-drafted brief citing fabricated court cases — a federal judge sanctioned them. Catch: AI generates confident, false citations with no built-in way to tell they're fake.*
Healthcare
IDx-DR became the first FDA-authorized autonomous AI diagnostic tool, screening for diabetic eye disease without a specialist reading the image. Catch: works only within a narrow, validated task — it defers ambiguous cases rather than guessing.*
Software & coding
A study testing GitHub Copilot on security-relevant coding tasks found about 40% of its suggested code contained exploitable vulnerabilities. Catch: AI coding assistants reproduce the flaws present in their training data.*
Accessibility (outside journalism)
Be My Eyes' "Be My AI," powered by GPT-4 with vision, describes photographed scenes for blind and low-vision users. Catch: documented hallucinations — confidently describing objects or text that aren't actually there.*
Logistics
UPS's ORION system optimizes delivery routes in real time, saving an estimated 100 million miles of driving a year. Catch: still struggles to match the tacit, local knowledge experienced drivers have.*
04

Not a Good Use Case (Yet)

  • Investigative reporting — AI can't call a source, dig through an archive, earn a whistleblower's trust, or read between the lines of what someone isn't saying.
  • Original breaking news — it synthesizes existing information; by the time it "knows" a story, the news is already public.
  • Opinion requiring deep expertise — AI can draft structure, but not credibility or unique perspective.
  • Stories requiring human experience — homelessness, racial justice: the reporting itself is human work.
  • Anything needing real-time accuracy — training data has a cutoff; for breaking facts, you need live sources.
05

The Pattern

Where AI actually works, and where it doesn't:

Works When

  • The task is repetitive and structured.
  • Input data is high-quality.
  • Speed matters more than perfection.
  • It augments human work, not replaces it.
  • You can verify the output yourself.
  • Mistakes are tolerable.

Doesn't Work When

  • The task needs original reporting or investigation.
  • Accuracy is non-negotiable.
  • It needs to replace human judgment.
  • Context and nuance are everything.
  • Readers need to trust a human made the call.
  • It requires real-world experience you haven't had.
06

The Practical Question

Before reaching for AI, ask yourself:

  • Is the task repetitive and well-structured, or does it need original judgment?*
  • Is my input data trustworthy and high-quality?*
  • Can I actually verify the output myself before it goes out?*
  • Would a mistake here be tolerable, or costly to my credibility?*
  • Am I augmenting my own work, or trying to replace judgment I should be exercising myself?*
  • Does this depend on real-time information, or a relationship/trust only a human can provide?
